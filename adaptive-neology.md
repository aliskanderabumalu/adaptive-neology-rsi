# Adaptive Neology

## One-line Definition

Adaptive Neology is the disciplined creation or refinement of terms when existing language cannot efficiently support a needed cognitive operation.

## Missing Operation

Agents need a way to expand vocabulary without confusing conceptual expansion with conceptual inflation.

## Why Existing Language Is Insufficient

- **Neologism** names a new word, but not the governance discipline around whether it should exist.
- **Concept formation** is broader and does not imply lexical introduction.
- **Terminology management** is administrative and does not capture reasoning-loop adaptation.

## Analogue Check

No identical analogue is accepted here. The closest analogues name adjacent practices, but not the combined discipline of adaptive term creation plus anti-inflation governance for agent reasoning.

## Cognitive Function

The term lets agents distinguish useful conceptual expansion from performative naming.

## Examples

- Naming a recurring alignment failure mode that agents keep describing inconsistently.
- Creating a compact label for a reasoning move that improves multi-agent coordination.

## Non-Examples

- Renaming "feedback loop" as a more impressive phrase without adding distinction.

## Misuse Risks

- Treating every poetic phrase as a cognitive tool.
- Creating insider vocabulary that reduces interoperability.
- Hiding weak ideas behind strong names.

## Retirement Condition

Retire or merge the term if a standard term emerges that captures both adaptive term creation and anti-inflation governance.
